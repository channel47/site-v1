# Building websites wasn't my job

Unpublished draft for editorial review.

---

My fiancée is a professional ballerina who also teaches private lessons. She had room for more students, but most people found her through word of mouth. I wanted to help her reach people who didn't already know someone she knew.

I'd been tinkering with Claude Code for months when I came across James Dickerson, who goes by [the Boring Marketer on Twitter](https://x.com/boringmarketer). He'd posted about building a website for Diesel Dudes with Claude Code. It was close enough to the things I was already trying in my free time that I could see myself doing it. He was using these tools to build something for someone in his life who needed it, and I had someone in mind.

In August 2025, I gave it a go. Three days later, Valle Ballet was live.

## Getting the site out there

The site was built around people in San Diego searching for private ballet lessons, with pages for the services and locations and a way to get in touch. I used Claude Code to build it in Astro, a framework for websites. Seeing the pages work on the first go was really rewarding, and I kept going until the less visible parts worked too.

One evening I stayed up after dinner getting the email forms set up. I eventually connected them to Slack so I'd know when someone sent an inquiry. The site needed to do more than look finished. It needed to help someone find a lesson and ask about it.

![Valle Ballet’s current homepage, with private lessons and trial bookings.](/posts/ballet-born-simple/valle-ballet.webp "screenshot")

I was enjoying the process enough to want to do it again. That same August, I started on a website for Born Simple, a food brand I'd worked with as a media buyer.

They were using Squarespace, and I didn't think the site was very good. Websites weren't what they'd known me for, but after Valle Ballet I was willing to step outside that role and see if I could build them a fresh one.

This site needed to answer a different set of questions. What was in each meal? How did you prepare it? Where could you buy it? I built out the product pages, nutrition information, recipes, and retailer links. I was still figuring things out as I went, but now I was doing it for a client.

![Born Simple’s current homepage, introducing the meals and where to buy them.](/posts/ballet-born-simple/born-simple.webp "screenshot")

## The first booking took longer

Building the ballet site took three days. Getting a student through it took longer. For weeks, nothing happened. With the Slack notifications in place, I more or less forgot about it.

Then inquiries started coming in. Five people reached out without booking. The sixth became her first client through the site.

At that first lesson, the student wouldn't let go of her dad's hand. By the end of the hour, she didn't want to leave.

I shared the story in James's community, and in January 2026 he posted my message on Twitter. A few months earlier, I'd been reading his example and thinking I could try something like that. Now he was sharing mine.

By the time I wrote that message, another lesson was scheduled, also from the website. There was someone on the other end of the forms I'd stayed up getting to work, asking my fiancée to teach them ballet.
