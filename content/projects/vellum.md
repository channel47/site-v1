I kept using Google Flow because I liked the good images so much. Its agent could take my references and help me find a direction for product shots I was excited to use. Getting them finished was less enjoyable. The packaging might be wrong, and when I asked for a correction, sometimes Flow simply ignored it.

The workspace itself made sense to me. The images sat in a grid, with a conversation beside them and very little else in the way. I could talk through what I wanted while looking at the work. I could also turn the agent off, write a prompt, and generate an image directly. I wanted to keep that arrangement and improve the parts that frustrated me, and by then, building my own version felt like something I could try.

I called it [Vellum](https://vellum.gallery/) and put it to work on [X-All](https://x-all.com/), a cleaning brand whose Shopify site I'd just rebuilt. The product pages needed images, so I had a reason to use the app while I was building it. References needed somewhere to live, products needed galleries, and I needed to know which images were still missing as the board filled up.

![X-All product-image studies in Vellum, with the prompt composer below the grid. The before-and-after compositions are generated concepts.](/posts/vellum/x-all-grid.webp "screenshot")

Much of the X-All board was blue tile, stainless steel, and water: scrubbers against bathroom walls, a spray bottle beside a stove, cleaning products around sinks. In one session I asked the agent, “Nice. What's next?” It checked the unfinished work and listed what remained, from products needing one more image to others with empty galleries. I asked it to pick one of the empty ones and make all three images.

It chose Glass Wipes. According to the agent, the packaging described wipes for eyeglasses and screens, so it took that batch out of the tiled bathrooms and into a setting with desks and monitors. The three images showed the blue pack beside a screen, a hand wiping a monitor, and someone cleaning a pair of glasses. They belonged together without repeating the setting of everything around them.

![The agent's conversation beside the X-All board, including its choice of Glass Wipes and the three images it generated.](/posts/vellum/x-all-agent.webp "screenshot")

That exchange gets at why I want an agent inside the workspace. I could ask a short question about what to do next because the products, images, and unfinished work were already there for it to consider. It could propose a batch and explain the choice, and I could look at the results in the same place as the conversation.

I've been building the organization around that kind of work. A Subject holds a product's notes and reference images, so they stay together as I make more images of it. A Placement records what I need for a particular use, such as the product's gallery. I can assign images to that gallery and export them together, while the agent has the references and style guides available as it helps fill the gaps.

There is plenty left to correct. In the same conversation about Glass Wipes, the agent reminded me that a garbage-disposal image still needed attention. It showed a yellow cleaning pod above a sink, with water running past it and blue tile behind. The pod was still enclosed in its clear blister packaging, and the drain collar looked industrial. The image fit the board at a glance, but I couldn't put it on a product page with those details wrong.

![The disposal image open for editing in Vellum. The yellow pod is still inside its clear blister packaging.](/posts/vellum/x-all-disposal-viewer.webp "screenshot")

Using the app has also made me notice problems in the app itself. Somewhere during the build, I lost direct generation. I'd made my own version of Flow and left out one of the things I liked about it. An agent is useful when I'm working out what to make; sometimes I already know, and I just want to write the prompt and see the image.

I brought direct generation back and made Generate the default. I can choose a model, attach references, and write the prompt myself. When I want help with a direction or the next batch, I switch to Agent. Whatever I've typed or attached stays with me, so I can move between the two as the work calls for it.
