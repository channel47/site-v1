I opened by tracing the lineage on air: we started these sessions with the basics, then built a brand voice, then a suite of related skills (customer research, personas, angles), and this lab took the next step in that chain, the **advertorial and landing page builder** skill. If the Creative Strategist session produced the angles, this session turned an angle into a page you can actually run traffic to.

## A real business as the case study

This one used a live community member's business: Yuli, who runs **Drink ELT**, an electrolyte-mix e-commerce brand in Malaysia, about 6 months old at the time. I'd run the Creative Strategist pipeline on her brand ahead of the session, and Claude independently surfaced a culturally specific angle I wouldn't have handed it: positioning against **Pocari Sweat**, the popular Asian electrolyte drink, on its high sugar content. It also found a second angle around athletes, specifically paddle players (big sport in the region) cramping mid-game.

For anyone new to the format, I explained what an advertorial is: the page that sits between the ad (Facebook, Google, LinkedIn) and the actual product or checkout page, priming the visitor with story and context before the sales ask.

We looked at real examples of three formats: an **editorial problem-solution** style (my generated Pocari Sweat comparison page for Yuli), a **reasons-why listicle** (a dog joint supplement brand, "5 reasons why your dog needs this," with tight page hygiene: no distracting header links, everything funneling to one CTA), and a **multi-step quiz funnel** (a collagen brand running roughly 25 to 30 sequential questions to build micro-commitment before an email gate and the final PDP handoff).

## What we built live

I built a second advertorial for Yuli on the cramping angle, the "dragged off the court" story, using the advertorial-builder skill. The skill references roughly 60 categorized real advertorial examples bundled inside it, which is how it picks the right format for the job instead of defaulting to one template.

Then the cross-tool part. I switched to **OpenAI Codex** (think Claude Code, but for ChatGPT) and used its native image generation to create real, on-brand product photography for the page. First pass didn't match Yuli's actual product, so I re-prompted it referencing the product photos on her real site, and that worked. The bigger point: the same project folder's skill files carried across Claude, Codex, and Cursor, each tool reading the shared context. Codex even prompts to migrate Claude-created skills when it detects them.

## The honest framing

AI-built advertorials get you roughly **80% of the way to something testable**, fast and cheap enough to run 3 or 4 angle variants before committing real design budget to the winner. That's the workflow, not "AI replaces your designer."

Yuli confirmed live that she'd launch and test the new pages, including a listicle-format version next. And the session pointed straight at the next natural lab topic: an ad static and creative generator skill, since testable creative is the other half of what these pages need to run as real ads.
