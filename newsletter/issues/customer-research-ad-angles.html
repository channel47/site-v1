In a discussion about packing cubes, one traveler described how they separate their clothes, then added, “I can select from one cube without disturbing anything else.”

That last sentence suggests something an ad could demonstrate. Take out the clothes you need and leave the rest of the bag packed.

Another traveler described moving to fewer, larger cubes because it made packing and unpacking easier. Same product category, but a different reason to buy. That ad might show the clothing together in one large compartment, with fewer separate pieces to handle.

If Claude summarizes both as “customers want convenience,” the summary omits the distinction that could change the ad.

I worked through three public discussions in the new article, keeping each quote attached as the research became two ad angles and a creative brief. The generated ad concepts show the different demonstrations. They also make the next questions easier to ask. Does the actual product fit the idea? Does the offer? What would the ad need to prove?
